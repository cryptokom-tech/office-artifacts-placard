# Local judging — Office Artifacts placard

Eight candidate lines were written in the build session and judged
pairwise against the published curator criteria, in order:
(1) a one-line placard for an ordinary office object, inventive and
funny; (2) a specific, surprising imagined history; (3) dry,
deadpan wit; (4) economy — every word earns its place, reads well
aloud; (5) label craft — date, material, provenance or donor used
to sell the joke.

## Candidates

- A1 (159 chars): Sticky Note, c. 2019. Paper and failing adhesive.
  An oracle whose prophecy detached within a day, as did the promise
  written on it. Donated by the Third Floor.
- A2 (156 chars): Sticky Note, c. 2019. Paper, adhesive failing. An
  oracle whose prophecy detached within a day, as did the promise
  written on it. Donated by the Third Floor.
- B (156 chars): Water Cooler, c. 2016. Plastic and standing water.
  A glugging shrine where the tribe traded news too dangerous for
  email. Gift of Facilities, in perpetuity.
- C (150 chars): Swivel Chair, c. 2014. Padded throne on castors.
  Its occupant turned slowly while saying 'per my last email,' a
  curse feared in the ruins. Gift of HR.
- D (151 chars): Microwave, c. 2018. Steel and old fish. A warming
  shrine; offerings were left despite three written warnings.
  Donated anonymously, for obvious reasons.
- E (152 chars): USB Drive, c. 2011. Plastic reliquary holding
  4,000 photographs and one presentation, none backed up. Recovered
  from a desk drawer, contents unreadable.
- F (130 chars): Keyboard, c. 2020. Plastic abacus; its space bar
  is polished by ten thousand deadlines. Gift of IT, who asked not
  to be contacted.
- G (141 chars): Lanyard, c. 2017. Woven cord of belonging. Its
  bearer was admitted everywhere except the meeting that mattered.
  Donor unknown; badge expired.

## Verdict

Winner: A2. The conceit carries two turns in one motion — the
adhesive fails and so does the promise — which is the most specific
imagined history in the set (criterion 2) delivered without strain
(criterion 3). "Paper, adhesive failing" is tighter catalogue prose
than A1's wording (criterion 4), and date, material and donor all
sell the joke rather than decorate it (criterion 5). B is the
runner-up: "news too dangerous for email" is sharp, but it is a
single turn. G is dry yet wastes its donor slot on "unknown". D's
reheated-fish joke is well worn, C leans on a common office phrase,
E has no donor, and F's polished space bar is generic.

The submitted line is A2 exactly: 156 characters, one line, UTF-8.
